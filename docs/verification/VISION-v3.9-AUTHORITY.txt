0.3 The Ten Laws
These ten laws are the spine of the product. If a build decision contradicts any of them, the build decision is wrong.
1.  One owner, many companies. You create companies; each company is completely independent of every other company — data, workers, branches, providers, money, branding and audit are never shared.
2.  The MD is the Head Office. Creating a company generates the MD Board and the MD's credentials. It never generates a "Head Office portal".
3.  The MD cannot work until he is secured. Password change, authenticator setup and verification, and profile completion are mandatory and blocking. The initial credential dies the moment it is used to change the password.
4.  Every worker is secured the same way. Every created worker must also change password and set up and verify an authenticator before doing anything, and the authenticator is what verifies their password change.
5.  Credentials are generated, never invented. Username is the full name; password is the at-sign plus the first name. They are handed over once, must work on first login, and stop working once the account is secured.
6. Money is never manually created or edited. No role can create or alter a payment, balance, posted allocation or financial record. A C.O. may enter allocation amounts only against a locked, already-verified payment; those entries are allocation instructions, not payment creation or editing. Financial corrections use an auditable reversal/correction workflow.
7.  Every worker is independent. Every role has its own complete workspace, its own powers and its own restrictions. No role is a copy of another, and no role borrows another role's pages.
8.  Head Office watches through the Branch Workplace, not through other people's passwords. Authorised Head Office roles enter a branch freely, see the branch's money and every worker's book; the worker is not told and is not disturbed.
9.  A customer is a borrower. Loans produce savings; savings alone is not a product. No savings-only customer, role or portal may exist.
10.  Nothing is fake. No placeholder balances, no duplicate customer records, no decorative numbers, no dead buttons. Every control that is visible must work.
11.  Every loan application is resumable and evidence-driven. Each stage is saved before Next; multiple live captures are allowed; unfinished work never disappears.
12.  Financial truth survives failure. Database records, evidence, audit history and backups are protected, recoverable, integrity-checked and never destructively deleted.

PART 1 — THE VISION
1.1 What Nexora is
Nexora is a cloud software platform for microfinance institutions, loan companies, cooperatives and credit organisations. You build it once and rent it out. One codebase serves unlimited companies, and each company experiences Nexora as if the software were built only for that company.
Nexora is not merely a digital ledger. It is a complete microfinance operating system: it runs the organisation that manages the loans — its Head Office, its branches, its staff hierarchy, its money movement and its accountability trail. The digitised collection ledger is one module inside that operating system, not the whole of it.
1.2 The business model, and what it means for the software
You are the owner. You rent the software to companies. That single sentence drives three engineering consequences:
1.  The Platform Owner portal must be complete. It is the factory floor: create companies, brand them, activate, suspend, reactivate, watch platform health, keep the platform audit trail, publish announcements, see platform-level activity. Nothing about running the business platform-side may be missing from it (Part 3 lists it in full).
2.  Everything a company touches must carry the company's identity. When a company logs in, the product must read as if it belongs to them: their name, their logo, their colour, their URL. The platform's own name appears only where no company can see it (the Platform Owner portal) and in owner-audit records.
3.  Company independence is a hard architectural border, not a setting. Company A must never be able to see, query, guess, influence or be affected by Company B — not in lists, not in reports, not in exports, not in notifications, not in provider credentials, not in webhooks, not in file storage, not even in error messages.
1.3 What Nexora is not
•  Not a savings product. There is no savings-only customer, no savings-only onboarding, no savings-only portal, no "Savings Officer" role invented for savings, and no virtual account generated merely to service a savings-only customer. Savings exists only as something the normal loan and payment lifecycle produces.
•  Not a cash product. No physical cash handling anywhere. No role records cash. Every naira that enters the system enters through a verified provider webhook.
•  Not a bank and not a wallet. Nexora does not hold customer money; it identifies, allocates and reports money that moved through the company's provider.
•  Not self-service for staff. No worker ever signs up. Accounts are created by authorised people and credentials are handed over.
1.4 Product goals
Six goals, in priority order:
1.  Remove paper ledgers and manual arithmetic from loan and collection operations.
2.  Give every company a fully isolated, fully branded workspace on shared infrastructure.
3.  Give every company complete operational visibility from Head Office down to a single customer's last payment, with no blind spots.
4.  Remove cash from the repayment process entirely, so every figure is bank-verified rather than human-reported.
5.  Let a company model its real staff hierarchy — Head Office roles and branch roles, each with its own workspace — instead of a fixed three-role hierarchy.
6.  Let branches scale from one to hundreds with no architectural change and no change in user experience.
1.5 The three governing principles
If money moves, Nexora must know — automatically, not because someone typed it in.
>
If an important action happens, Nexora must know who performed it, from where, and when.
>
If something does not match — a payment, a balance, a webhook, a ledger total — Nexora must expose the discrepancy rather than silently resolve or hide it.
These three sentences are why cash is banned (Part 10), why every state change carries an audit entry (Part 14), and why reconciliation exceptions are a first-class object in the data model (Part 11.5).
1.6 Company independence law
•  Every company-owned table carries a mandatory indexed company_id. No table containing customers, loans, workers, payments, branches, providers, audit entries, files or reports may lack it.
•  Every branch-owned table additionally carries branch_id, enforced the same way, so one branch can never silently return another branch's rows inside the same company.
•  Scoping is enforced at the data-access layer, not only in screen logic, so a mistake in one screen cannot leak data.
•  Provider credentials, webhook secrets, webhook URLs, webhook logs and virtual accounts are all company-and-branch scoped. Two companies may both use Monnify and must be unable to reach each other's credentials, webhook endpoints, contracts or customer accounts.
•  Every automated test suite must include an explicit cross-tenant attack test: authenticated as company A, attempt to read and write company B's records by guessed id, and prove the attempt fails and is audit-logged.

Rules:
RULE 2.3.1 — The company slug is fixed when the company is created, is unique platform-wide, and is derived from the company name (lowercase, punctuation stripped). The company's own optional domain is pointed at the same application and never changes the internal slug.
RULE 2.3.2 — A branch slug is derived from the branch name at creation, deduplicated inside the company (for example ikirun, then ikirun2). The slug is cosmetic: routing and permissions always use the immutable branch id, so renaming a branch never breaks permissions.
RULE 2.3.3 — Branch URLs never grant Head Office access, and the company URL never grants branch-worker access, unless the person's own credentials say so.
2.4 The credential-to-portal resolution law
The same URL, many logins, no menus to pick from.
RULE 2.4.1 — After a credential pair is accepted at a company or branch URL, the system resolves company, branch (if any), worker, role(s) and permissions by itself, then opens the correct portal directly.
WHY — a worker must never be asked "which company?", "which branch?", "which role?", because that is how mistakes and impersonation happen.
ENFORCEMENT — the URL supplies the scope; the credential identifies the person; the role assignment supplies the portal. A branch URL login by a worker with no assignment at that branch is rejected with a neutral error and is audit-logged.
PROOF — one branch URL, three different credentials (Branch Manager, Collection Officer, Senior Collection Officer) must open three different portals with three different permission sets, with no branch or role selection screen anywhere in the flow.
RULE 2.4.2 — A person holding more than one role at the same context gets a role lens switch inside the portal ("Viewing as ..."), never a second login and never a second URL.
RULE 2.4.3 — The wrong password never reveals which field was wrong, whether the user exists, or which company exists.

RULE 3.6.2 — The Platform Owner cannot, by default: read any company's customers, loans, payments, savings, ledger, accounting or reconciliation data; read any individual worker's performance figures; edit, reverse or act on any company financial record; read a company's own internal audit trail; or sign into a company portal as a company user.
RULE 3.6.3 — Support access, when needed, is its own pathway: it requires an explicit request naming the company and a reason, it is time-bound and expires automatically, every action taken inside it is written to both the platform audit trail and the company's own audit trail, and it renders with a persistent banner naming the company and the session expiry. It is never reachable by ordinary clicking through the company detail pages.
WHY — trust. Companies must know that no one, including the platform owner, can silently read their books.
PROOF — attempt to reach any customer, loan or payment from every Platform Owner page: no route exists; then open a support session and prove the company's audit trail received an entry.
3.7 Platform audit and notifications
RULE 3.7.1 — Every platform-level action is written to the platform audit trail with: actor, timestamp in server time, action type, the company it concerns, before value, after value, session and IP metadata, and reason where the action type requires it (suspension, support access, role catalogue changes).
3.8 Multi-company concurrency and platform execution capacity
RULE 3.8.1 — Nexora is a multi-tenant platform and must be architected to operate many companies concurrently. Company A, B, C, D, E and further companies may have users, branches, searches, reports, notifications, provider operations, loan workflows and other work executing at the same time. One company's workload must not require unrelated companies to wait unnecessarily.
RULE 3.8.2 — Company isolation is also an execution boundary. Every asynchronous job, queued operation, provider request, webhook processing task, notification task, report task and other background execution context must retain and enforce the relevant company_id and branch_id where applicable. Execution must resolve the correct tenant and scope before reading secrets, data or configuration.
RULE 3.8.3 — The platform must support concurrent operations without serialising unrelated companies through one global worker, one global provider session, one global lock or one shared mutable tenant context. Where asynchronous execution is appropriate, jobs/workers/queues must permit independent company operations to proceed concurrently while respecting database consistency, provider limits and resource limits.
RULE 3.8.4 — A slow, failed, rate-limited or unavailable provider for Company A must not corrupt, misroute or unnecessarily block unrelated provider operations for Company B, C, D or other companies. Provider-specific retries, backoff, rate limits and failure states must remain scoped to the affected operation/provider/company.
RULE 3.8.5 — Concurrent execution must be safe under simultaneous requests. Operations that can be retried or delivered more than once must be idempotent where required. Database transactions, uniqueness constraints, locking and state transitions must prevent duplicate financial records, duplicate virtual accounts, duplicate webhook effects and other race-condition corruption.
RULE 3.8.6 — Platform capacity must scale with increasing companies, users, branches and simultaneous operations through the execution architecture rather than by changing business rules or creating a separate application for each company. The system must support horizontal/background execution scaling, bounded resource use, queue backpressure where needed, observability and graceful failure without cross-tenant contamination.
RULE 3.8.7 — The virtual-account creation flow is a mandatory concurrency example. If seven C.O.s from seven different companies/branches disburse loans at the same time, Nexora must be able to process the seven operations independently: resolve each company and branch, select that branch's configured payment provider, retrieve only that company's/branch's credentials, determine the provider's required customer details, send the correct request to the correct provider, receive the provider response, and persist the returned virtual account against the correct customer and loan.
RULE 3.8.8 — Concurrent virtual-account creation must preserve all existing loan/disbursement atomicity and provider rules. A failure, timeout or retry in one operation must not cause another company's virtual account, loan, customer, provider credentials or financial records to be attached to the wrong tenant.
PROOF — The acceptance suite must include concurrent multi-company scenarios, including at minimum simultaneous loan disbursements/virtual-account creation across multiple companies and providers, with assertions for correct routing, tenant isolation, idempotency, failure isolation, persistence, auditability and absence of cross-company state contamination. The test must use separate company/branch/customer/loan identities and verify every resulting record against its originating operation.
RULE 3.7.2 — Platform audit entries are append-only. There is no edit and no delete action anywhere in the portal.
RULE 3.7.3 — The Platform Owner is notified of: new company created, company stalled in setup beyond a threshold, company suspended or reactivated, platform health threshold breaches, webhook pipeline health breaches, and every support access session opened or closed.

PART 5 — THE CREDENTIAL AND ROLE LAW (EVERY USER, EVERY ROLE)
5.1 The username law
RULE 5.1.1 — The username of every worker is the worker's full name, exactly as captured, in normal spelling and spacing.
			Example
			Username

			A worker named Cornelius Adedeji Victor
			Cornelius Adedeji Victor

			A worker named Victor Adeyemi
			Victor Adeyemi

RULE 5.1.2 — The username is the software's output, not a field a human invents. Nobody types a username. The creating person types the name once, and the software derives the username from it.
RULE 5.1.3 — Spaces are allowed, capitalisation is preserved as captured, and neither the phone number nor the email is ever used as a login identity.
RULE 5.1.4 — Uniqueness is enforced inside the company (and inside the platform for the Platform Owner). A full name must be unique as a login identity inside a company. If a proposed worker name already exists, the creation flow must stop and require the authorised creator to correct the person's full name before the account is created. The software must never append a suffix, silently alter the full-name username, or create two accounts that authenticate with the same username and initial password. The one thing that may never happen is a generated credential that does not log in.
5.2 The password law
RULE 5.2.1 — The initial password of every worker is the at-sign followed by the first name, with the first letter capitalised — for example @Cornelius for Cornelius Adedeji Victor.
RULE 5.2.2 — The initial password must genuinely authenticate. The generation routine and the authentication routine must be the same code path, so that a generated password cannot fail to work. It is stored only as a hash, and the one-time panel is produced from the generated value before hashing.
RULE 5.2.3 — The initial password is a one-time credential, not a password:
1.  It works for exactly one successful login.
2.  It must be changed during that login, before anything else.
3.  The moment the password change succeeds, the initial credential is destroyed and can never authenticate again.
4.  If the ritual is abandoned mid-way, the account returns to "ritual required" and the same initial credential may still be used (until it expires), because the change did not complete.
5.  It expires if unused within the company's configured window (default 7 days), after which only an authorised person (HR, MD, or the Branch Workplace for its branch workers) can issue a replacement initial credential, and that issuance is audit-logged.
WHY the design accepts a guessable initial password — you asked for credentials a manager can hand over verbally, on paper, or by phone, to workers who often have no reliable email: full name plus @FirstName is that credential. The risk is contained by three hard rules together: it is one-time, the account is unusable until the ritual is completed, and every serious action afterwards requires the authenticator.
PROOF — create a worker, log in with @FirstName, complete the ritual, then attempt to log in again with @FirstName: the attempt must fail.
RULE 5.2.4 — The same law applies to the MD, to the Platform Owner, to Head Office roles, to branch roles, to Custom Roles and to Customer Portal access: full name as username, at-sign plus first name as the initial password.
5.3 The mandatory ritual — for everyone, not only the MD
RULE 5.3.1 — Every worker account, in every role, in every company, must complete the following before it can do anything at all:
1.  Change the password. The new password must satisfy the company's password policy, must not equal the initial credential, must not equal the username or the person's name, and must be confirmed twice. The password change is verified with the authenticator (see step 2 to 3): the person must enter a live authenticator code to prove the change is really theirs.
2.  Set up the authenticator. The system shows an enrolment secret and QR code, and the person adds it to their authenticator application.
3.  Verify the authenticator. The person enters a live code. Setup alone is not enough; verification is what binds the authenticator to the account. Until verification succeeds, the account is not secured.
4.  Complete the profile. Full name and passport. For all workers this is mandatory; for the MD it is mandatory before anything else opens.
RULE 5.3.2 — The authenticator becomes the factor that authorises future sensitive actions: password changes, authenticator removal or re-setup, credential re-issuance, portfolio hold and portfolio transfer, provider changes, and high-value reversals.
RULE 5.3.3 — Until the ritual completes, the account can reach nothing except the ritual screens and sign-out, and every attempt to reach anything else is refused and audit-logged. This applies identically to the MD, the GM, the HR Manager, the Branch Manager, a Collection Officer, and any Custom Role holder.
RULE 5.3.4 — No worker may ever be marked "exempt" from the ritual, no customer-facing or temporary account bypasses it, and no configuration option may disable it.
WHY — the initial password is guessable by design (5.2), so the ritual is the entire reason the credential model is safe. Removing it for some roles would make those roles the weakest door into the company.
5.4 The credential lifecycle states
Every worker account is always in exactly one of these states. The state is enforced server-side and is visible to the MD and HR.
			State
			Meaning

RULE 5.4.1 — Every transition between these states is audit-logged with actor, reason, timestamp, and the before and after state.
5.5 The role independence law
RULE 5.5.1 — Every role is independent. A role is defined by: its purpose, its portal, its own pages, its own dashboard blocks, its own powers, its own restrictions, its own notifications, and its own performance scope. No role shares another role's component tree, and no role is described as "the same as X but smaller".
RULE 5.5.2 — Concretely, this means:
•  A Cash and Bank Reconciliation Officer does not get the Auditor's pages, and the Auditor does not get the Finance Manager's posting pages.
•  A Deputy Branch Manager has his own portal with his own superset/subset of branch powers, not a renamed Branch Manager screen.
•  A Custom Role is assembled from a permission vocabulary, and once assembled it behaves exactly like a built-in role with respect to scope, ritual, audit and restrictions.
RULE 5.5.3 — Where two roles legitimately need the same capability (for example, both the Auditor and the MIS Officer need the company performance view), the capability is a shared service that both roles are granted read access to — never a copy-pasted screen, and never a silent permission handover from one role to another.
RULE 5.5.4 — Effective permissions for a person holding more than one role are the union of the grants of all currently active assignments, each evaluated at its own scope. There is no "highest role wins" and no silent downgrade.
RULE 5.5.5 — A restriction attached to one role does not cancel an action separately granted by another active assignment of the same person; restrictions are evaluated per assignment, and every action records which role assignment was used to perform it.
5.6 The money non-editing law
RULE 5.6.1 — No role can create, edit, delete or adjust a payment, a repayment allocation, a savings figure, a loan balance, a ledger line, an accounting entry or a virtual account. There is no exception for the MD, the Platform Owner, Finance, the Auditor or support access.
RULE 5.6.2 — Money enters the system in exactly one way: a verified provider webhook, processed by the payment pipeline, allocated by the C.O. Payment Allocation workflow, written to the ledger, and reflected into performance and accounting. The verification and recording stages of this chain are automatic; C.O. allocation is a required human action.
RULE 5.6.3 — When a financial correction is required, the system uses a controlled reversal/correction workflow that creates a new linked, audit-logged record and leaves the original untouched forever. Finance may investigate, reconcile and prepare a correction request, but Finance does not edit or directly post a financial correction. Approval authority for a financial reversal/correction is restricted to the MD, GM and authorised Auditor according to the company's configured control policy. The posting service creates the new financial record after required approval; no person edits the original.
RULE 5.6.4 — The Auditor, the MD and the Platform Owner (inside an authorised support session) can read everything, but reading never becomes writing. No screen anywhere contains a control that edits money.

5.8 When a worker leaves or is sacked — freeze, hold and transfer
This section answers one question precisely: a worker has been sacked or has stopped working with us; how do we stop him logging in, and how does his book keep working? The answer has two possible outcomes, and HR (or the MD) chooses one.
RULE 5.8.1 — On every worker account, in the account settings area, there are two controls. These controls are visible only to HR and the MD. They are not visible to the worker when he views his own profile, not visible to a Branch Manager, not visible to Finance and not visible to the Auditor (who may read the resulting records but cannot operate the controls).
RULE 5.8.2 — Option one: Keep Portfolio On Hold. What it does, in order:
1.  All login as that worker is blocked immediately: the password stops authenticating, the authenticator is frozen, all existing sessions are invalidated, and the worker cannot sign in from the branch URL or company URL. Only authorised Head Office users may work with the held portfolio through their own accounts and the Branch Workplace/appropriate Head Office workspace.
2.  Only Head Office users can work with that book — the MD, HR, and the authorised Head Office roles that can read branch work.
3.  Money can still be received. Holding a portfolio does not stop customers paying. Payments continue to arrive through the provider webhook and are recorded.
4.  Money received while the portfolio is on hold is not allocated as if a normal Collection Officer were running the book. It lands in awaiting allocation guidance — held, visible and flagged — waiting for a Collection Officer (or another worker) to take over the portfolio and complete the allocation.
5.  Nothing is deleted: no customer, no group, no loan and no payment is touched.
WHY this option exists — sometimes the company has not yet decided who takes over the book, or the worker is under investigation, or the worker is on long leave. The customers must not be abandoned and the money must not be lost.
RULE 5.8.3 — Option two: Change Worker On This Portfolio. What it does, in order:
1.  HR (or the MD) selects the option and confirms it with a reason.
2.  The system immediately, in the background, with no front-end delay deactivates the password on that account, deactivates and unlinks the authenticator so it stops working at once, invalidates all sessions and freezes the account — writing a security audit entry for each step.
3.  The system then opens the same flow used to create a new worker, and the operator enters the new worker's details.
4.  The system generates the new worker's credentials (username = full name, initial password = at-sign plus first name) and shows the one-time credential panel.
5.  The new worker logs in and must complete the same mandatory Credential Ritual — change password with authenticator verification, set up authenticator, verify, complete profile — before anything opens.
6.  When the new worker's portal opens, the portfolio is already there and already working: the same customers, groups, loans, schedules and records. If money is sitting in awaiting-allocation-guidance, it is visible immediately and he allocates it.
7.  The new worker cannot edit any money that was received. Money received is view-only, forever.
RULE 5.8.4 — The portfolio belongs to the seat, not to the person. Transfers preserve the full history: the new worker sees what was received before he arrived, and the audit trail preserves exactly which worker was responsible for which period.
RULE 5.8.5 — The previous worker's record is never deleted. It becomes historical, frozen or terminated, with role assignments end-dated, so every loan, payment, allocation and approval he ever touched remains attributable forever.
RULE 5.8.6 — Neither option may delete, mutate, renumber, re-date or tidy up any financial record, customer record or audit entry.
5.9 HR's authority over worker records
RULE 5.9.1 — HR has unlimited authority over worker records, and it is deliberately wide, because HR owns the workforce:
			HR can
			HR cannot

			Create workers, Head Office and branch, with generated credentials
			Edit, create or delete any financial record

			See money-entry screens (none exist)

RULE 5.9.2 — When the company wants a new person to take over an existing portfolio, HR does not overwrite the former worker's identity. HR starts the Change Worker On This Portfolio flow, creates a separate worker identity for the new person, and assigns the existing portfolio/seat to that new worker. The portfolio, customers, groups and financial history remain intact; only the active portfolio-holder assignment changes. Fresh credentials are generated from the new worker's name.
RULE 5.9.3 — Every HR action in this section is audit-logged with actor, reason, before and after values and timestamp, and the MD is notified of holds and transfers.
5.10 The audit of account state — the mandatory list
RULE 5.10.1 — Each of the following must produce an audit entry, without exception, in every company: credential issued; credential consumed by password change; credential expired; password changed; password change denied; authenticator set up; authenticator verified; authenticator frozen; authenticator removed; profile completed; profile edited; passport replaced; worker created; worker edited; role assignment created, changed or ended; worker suspended, reactivated or terminated; portfolio put on hold; portfolio hold released; portfolio transferred; login succeeded; login failed; lockout triggered; and credential re-issued.
RULE 5.10.2 — Security audit entries are immutable, are never editable anywhere in the product, and are readable by the MD, by HR for people events, by the Auditor, and by the Platform Owner only through the authorised support path.

PART 8 — PAYMENT PROVIDERS: THE DYNAMIC PROVIDER REGISTRY
This part is written from research into how Nigerian payment providers actually work, because you asked for the provider screen to change according to what the chosen provider actually requires. The research conclusion is simple, and it changes the design: Nigerian providers do not require the same fields, so a fixed provider form would be wrong for almost every provider.
8.1 What the research found
The table below is what the builder must design the registry around. The field counts are the fields a company must supply to set the provider up, beyond anything the platform supplies automatically.
			Provider
			What a company must provide to set it up
			Customer-level KYC the provider demands for a virtual account
			Webhook security the platform must implement

			Monnify (Moniepoint group)
			API key, secret key, contract code, and the merchant's settlement or wallet reference — roughly two to four fields depending on structure
			The customer's BVN or NIN is required; both are required for the maximum transaction limit, one is enough for a limited-limit account
			HMAC-SHA512 of the request body using the merchant secret key, compared against the provider's signature header; also restrict by the provider's published source IP

			Flutterwave
			Public key, secret key, secret hash, and a preferred bank code for the account type — roughly three to four fields
			Static virtual accounts require first name, last name, email and either BVN or NIN; dynamic accounts require an email and an amount
			Compare the provider's verification header against the configured secret hash on every webhook, and re-query the provider to confirm amount, currency and reference before giving value

			Paystack
			Secret key and a preferred bank provider slug for the dedicated account — roughly two fields
			The customer record must exist first with a name and email, and the account is then attached to that customer
			Signature header computed as HMAC-SHA512 of the raw body with the secret key, compared on every webhook

			Squad (HabariPay, GTCO)
			Secret key, a settlement (beneficiary) account, and a virtual account prefix agreed with the provider before go-live — roughly three fields
			Strict validation: first name, last name, middle name, mobile number, date of birth, email, gender, address, BVN (compulsory) and a unique customer identifier; the account is only created when the BVN data matches the supplied name, date of birth, gender and phone
			Versioned webhook validation: either a hash comparison or decryption of the encrypted body, depending on the version the merchant is provisioned on

			OPay
			Merchant id, API keys and a callback URL; some products add asymmetric-key encryption
			Product dependent, with the provider's own KYC rules applying
			Callback verification per the provider's merchant specification, with the platform holding the keys

			PalmPay
			Merchant id and keys, with merchant onboarding completed in the provider's business dashboard first
			Product dependent, with the provider's own KYC rules applying
			Callback verification per the merchant specification

			Moniepoint (direct)
			Merchant credentials from the business dashboard, plus account or terminal structure details for the collection product
			Product dependent; business onboarding is KYC-heavy on the company side
			Provider-specified callback verification

RULE 8.1.1 — None of that is hard-coded into screens. The provider registry (8.3) stores, per provider, exactly which fields it needs, how the person obtains each field, how each field is validated, how the connection is tested, how the webhook is secured, and what the provider can and cannot do. The screen is then generated from the registry.
RULE 8.1.2 — The registry must ship with Monnify, Flutterwave, Paystack, Squad, OPay and PalmPay pre-configured, plus a "generic or manual provider" entry for a company using something not listed, so no company is ever blocked.
RULE 8.1.3 — Because customer-level KYC requirements differ — some providers require a BVN or NIN to create a customer's virtual account — the customer record must hold the KYC data a provider requires, and the provider's requirement must be checked before an attempt is made, so the failure is explained instead of discovered.
8.2 The screen follows the provider
RULE 8.2.1 — When a company chooses a provider on the branch's provider page, the form re-renders to that provider's own requirement set. The company must never see fields that the chosen provider does not need, and must never be missing a field the provider requires.
			Chosen provider
			The form shows

			Monnify
			Contract code, API key, secret key, settlement or wallet reference; plus the per-customer KYC requirement notice

			Flutterwave
			Public key, secret key, secret hash, preferred bank code

			Paystack
			Secret key, preferred bank provider

			Squad
			Secret key, settlement account number, virtual account prefix

			OPay
			Merchant id, API key, secret key (or merchant keys), callback path confirmation

			PalmPay
			Merchant id, API key, secret key

			Generic or manual
			A free field set the company defines, with the same help, validation and test-connection machinery

RULE 8.2.2 — Every field on the form carries, inline and always visible on demand:
1.  What it is — a plain-language description.
2.  How to get it — the exact place in the provider's own dashboard where the value is found (for example "Settings, then API keys and webhooks" for one provider, "Developer, then API keys" for another), and what the value looks like.
3.  Whether it is a secret — secrets are masked after entry, are never shown again in full, are never logged, are encrypted at rest, and are excluded from every export and every audit "after value" field.
4.  Whether it can be tested — a per-field or per-provider test where the provider supports one.
RULE 8.2.3 — The builder must not invent the guidance: it must be written from each provider's own current documentation, and the registry must make it easy to update that guidance later without a code change.
8.3 The provider registry data model
RULE 8.3.1 — The registry is data, not code. Per provider it stores:
			Element
			What it holds

			Provider identity
			Name, code, logo, documentation link, supported country and currency, status

			Requirement set
			The ordered list of fields the company must supply, each with: label, description, how to obtain it, whether it is secret, whether it is required, its validation rule, its example value, and whether it is customer-specific or company-specific

			Virtual account descriptor

RULE 8.3.2 — A branch's own provider configuration record stores: the branch, the provider, the field values, the activation state, the authorisation state, the webhook endpoint the platform generated for that branch, the webhook secret reference, the last successful connection test, the last webhook received, and the health counters (successes, failures, retries).
RULE 8.3.3 — Provider credentials are stored encrypted, are never returned to any screen in readable form after saving except through an explicit, audit-logged reveal that the MD may authorise, and are never included in exports, logs, error messages or support data.
8.4 Adding, editing, removing and changing a provider
RULE 8.4.1 — The full flow, from the Branch Workplace:
Branch Workplace -> Provider
  1. Choose provider (or choose generic)
  2. The form renders exactly that provider's required fields
  3. Each field shows what it is and how to get it
  4. Enter the values
  5. Run Test connection
        - success  -> continue
        - failure  -> show the provider's own error, explain it in plain language, and block activation
  6. Save
  7. Authorisation:
        - if the actor is the MD  -> the provider becomes Active immediately
        - if the actor is anyone else (Deputy MD, GM, Finance)
              -> the configuration is saved in "awaiting MD authorisation"
              -> the MD receives an authorisation notification with the details and the reason
              -> the MD approves (provider becomes Active) or rejects (provider stays inactive, with the reason recorded)
  8. On becoming Active, the system automatically binds the branch's webhook to that provider (8.5)
  9. Audit entries are written for: configuration saved, test run and its result, authorisation requested, authorised or rejected, provider activated or deactivated, webhook bound
RULE 8.4.2 — Editing a provider follows the same flow, with the same authorisation rule: the MD's own edit applies immediately; anyone else's edit waits for the MD. Already-stored secrets are not re-shown; the editor replaces them only if new values are entered.
RULE 8.4.3 — Removing or deactivating a provider follows the same authorisation rule, and must warn explicitly, in the confirmation, what will stop working: no new virtual accounts can be created for that branch, and existing customer accounts with that provider may stop receiving payments.
RULE 8.4.4 — Adding another provider alongside an existing one is allowed where the company's plan allows it, and each provider keeps its own configuration, its own webhook binding and its own health record. The branch must always know which provider is primary for creating new virtual accounts.
RULE 8.4.5 — No approval of a provider change may be given on behalf of the MD by anyone. The authorisation is performed inside the MD's own authenticated session, with his role assignment recorded.
8.5 The webhook law — automatic, and nobody's job to type
RULE 8.5.1 — The moment a provider becomes Active for a branch, the system must:
1.  Generate the branch's webhook endpoint, uniquely and non-guessably, scoped to that company, that branch and that provider.
2.  Register it with the provider where the provider supports programmatic registration, or present it to the operator with a copy action and provider-specific instructions where the provider requires manual registration in its dashboard. In both cases the endpoint is generated by the system, never typed by a person.
3.  Bind incoming webhooks from that endpoint to that branch, that provider and that company, automatically.
4.  Store the webhook secret or signature scheme reference for that binding.
5.  Start verifying every incoming webhook with the provider's scheme (signature comparison, hash comparison, or decryption, depending on the provider), reject anything that fails verification, and log the rejection.
RULE 8.5.2 — Nobody — not the MD, not Finance, not IT — types or chooses a webhook URL or branch binding. IT may set or rotate provider credentials when carrying out the authorised provider-credential configuration described below, but IT never chooses the webhook destination. The binding is a system fact, derived from the provider configuration of that branch.
WHY — you said it plainly: this must happen by itself. A manually chosen webhook is how money gets credited to the wrong branch, and the operator would never be able to see the mistake.
ENFORCEMENT — the branch and the provider are resolved from the endpoint and the verified payload, not from a field a human filled in.
PROOF — configure a provider on branch A, then send a signed test payload: the payment must land in branch A's records. Change the payload's customer reference to a customer of branch B: the payment must be rejected as not belonging to that endpoint, and no record may be written to branch B.
RULE 8.5.3 — Every webhook is idempotent: replaying the same event must not create a second payment. Duplicate detection uses the provider's own transaction reference.
RULE 8.5.4 — Every webhook is stored raw before processing, so any disputed payment can be re-examined exactly as it arrived.
RULE 8.5.4A — Before a provider transaction becomes an eligible payment, Nexora must verify, from the authenticated provider payload and provider state where available: webhook authenticity/signature, provider transaction reference, idempotency, transaction validity/status, company binding, branch binding, customer identity or virtual-account match, relevant active loan/customer relationship, and whether the transaction is refunded, reversed, disputed or chargebacked. A transaction that fails or cannot yet satisfy these checks is recorded in an explicit exception/awaiting state and cannot enter the normal C.O. allocation queue.
RULE 8.5.5 — Failed verification, failed processing, duplicate detection and retry exhaustion all raise a reconciliation exception (Part 11.5) rather than disappearing silently.
RULE 8.5.6 — The platform must never rely only on webhooks: a scheduled polling or reconciliation job must periodically confirm that every provider transaction matches a recorded payment, and must raise an exception for anything found only on the provider side.
8.6 What must never happen with providers
RULE 8.6.1 — None of the following may exist in the product:
1.  A manually typed webhook URL, or a screen that lets a person choose which branch a webhook belongs to.
2.  A shared provider credential between two companies, or between two branches of the same company unless the company deliberately configures one merchant account for several branches and the platform records that fact explicitly.
3.  A provider form with a fixed field set that ignores the chosen provider.
4.  A provider activation that skips the connection test.
5.  A provider change by a role outside MD, Deputy MD, GM and Finance.
6.  A provider change by a non-MD user that becomes active without the MD's authorisation.
7.  Provider secrets in logs, error messages, exports, notifications, screenshots in support tools or audit "after" values.
8.  A silent provider swap: replacing a provider without an audit entry, without an MD authorisation where required, and without informing the affected branches.
9. A payment written into the system by anything other than a verified provider transaction processed through the system's authorised reconciliation/integration service. A human may investigate or reconcile an exception, but cannot type a payment into existence.
8.7 What a provider change does to existing customers
RULE 8.7.1 — When a branch changes provider:
1.  Existing virtual accounts created with the old provider remain valid and remain recorded against their customers. They are never deleted.
2.  If the old provider's accounts stop working, the platform must flag every affected customer, and the branch must regenerate virtual accounts with the new provider for those customers. Regeneration links the new account to the same customer record; the old account's status becomes Replaced.
3.  A customer must never end up with two active accounts and no indication of which one to pay into: the platform always shows exactly one current account per customer, with history retained.
4.  No customer record is duplicated because a provider changed.
5.  The change is audit-logged, and the MD is notified of both the change and of any customers left needing a new account.
8.8 Provider health and the IT duty
RULE 8.8.1 — The IT and System Administrator owns the technical provider-credential and integration duty: provider API credentials, integration settings, webhook health, webhook success rate, failures, retries, last successful webhook, last settlement, connection-test status and provider downtime. IT may enter, replace and rotate provider credentials through the provider-credential controls, but the action is always audit-logged and, where the underlying provider configuration requires MD authorisation, it remains subject to the MD authorisation rule. IT cannot enter, edit, allocate or correct money.
RULE 8.8.2 — IT cannot resolve a money exception, allocate a payment, or decide what a payment should be applied to. Keeping the pipeline healthy and resolving what the pipeline surfaces are deliberately different jobs.

PART 9 — CUSTOMERS
9.1 What a customer is, and what a customer is not
RULE 9.1.1 — A customer is a borrower. The customer journey is: registered by a Collection Officer, taken through the lending lifecycle, disbursed, given a virtual account for repayment, and given Customer Portal access.
RULE 9.1.2 — The following must not exist anywhere in the product, in any table, screen, route, role or report:
•  A savings-only customer, or a customer created purely to save.
•  Savings-only onboarding, a savings-only registration flow, or a savings-only portal.
•  A "Savings Officer" role, or any invented role whose purpose is savings.
•  A virtual account generated merely to support a savings-only customer.
•  A separate customer record created because the customer took a loan or received portal access.
RULE 9.1.3 — Savings is still part of this product, but only as an outcome of the normal loan and payment lifecycle: the repayment schedule may define a configured savings component/split where the loan product says so, the C.O. Payment Allocation workflow records the actual savings portion when a verified payment is allocated and posted, and the customer's record and portal show Savings Achieved. Savings is never a product on its own and never a reason to create a customer.
WHY — you were explicit: this software is for companies giving out loans; a client may pay a repayment together with savings, but there is nothing like a savings-only customer.
RULE 9.1.4 — A customer's record is the single source of truth. A loan, its schedule, its payments, its virtual account and its portal access all hang off that one customer record. No duplicates, ever, under any flow.
9.2 Customer registration and the customer profile
RULE 9.2.1 — A customer is registered by a Collection Officer (or another role with equivalent customer-creation permission) inside his own portal, at the point of meeting the customer.
RULE 9.2.2 — Registration captures a complete profile. The following must all be captured, and the profile must be complete before a loan can be disbursed:
			Group
			Contents

			Identity
			Full name, gender, date of birth, marital status where relevant, mother's maiden name where relevant

			Photograph
			The customer's face, captured live at registration and again at loan application (9.4)

			Contact
			Phone number, alternative phone, email where available

			Address
			Residential address, plus business or work address, with the ability to record a location pin

			Identification
			Identification type and number, and any KYC document the company requires

			KYC data required by the branch's provider
			BVN or NIN where the branch's provider requires it for the customer's virtual account

			Business and income
			Occupation, business type, business address, estimated income, where the company requires it

			Next of kin
			Name, relationship, phone — required

			Guarantor
			Name, relationship, phone, address, and the guarantor's live-captured face (9.4)

			Branch and assignment
			Branch, and the Collection Officer the customer is assigned to

			Group
			Optional at registration; a customer may be placed in a group later

			Bank account
			Optional, for reference

			Photo and document record
			Every image is stored with its capture metadata (9.4)

RULE 9.2.3 — Editing an existing customer's profile is allowed for authorised roles (the responsible Collection Officer, the Branch Manager, Customer Service, Credit and HR-adjacent roles per permission), but no edit may remove or alter a financial record, a payment, an allocation or a captured face image. Face images are immutable: a new capture creates a new record; the old one is retained.
RULE 9.2.4 — Every customer profile change is audit-logged with before and after values and the actor.
9.3 Groups
RULE 9.3.1 — Customers are organised into branch-level groups, created and managed by Collection Officers (or a role with equivalent scope). A group is a convenience wrapper for field work: it groups the Collection Officer's customers for collection rounds, reporting and monitoring.
RULE 9.3.2 — A group never owns money. Every loan, schedule, payment, allocation and savings figure belongs to the individual customer. A group has no balance of its own and no ledger of its own, and closing or renaming a group never changes a member's records.
RULE 9.3.3 — A customer belongs to exactly one branch, and may belong to a group within that branch.
9.4 The face-capture law (loans and guarantors)
This section exists because of a real fraud you described: a Collection Officer applying for a loan for a customer who was not there, using a photograph of the customer's passport.
RULE 9.4.1 — When a customer applies for a loan, the applicant's face is captured live, from the camera inside the application itself. There is no passport-number field, no passport-photo upload, and no "choose a file from the phone" action anywhere in the loan application.
RULE 9.4.2 — The following are prohibited in the loan application and in the guarantor step, without exception:
1.  Uploading a photograph from the device gallery.
2.  Uploading a file of any kind as the applicant's or guarantor's face.
3.  Entering a passport number as a substitute for the person being present.
4.  Reusing a previously captured image from another application or another customer.
5.  Any "skip" or "continue without capture" path.
6.  Capturing from a camera that is not the device's live camera.
RULE 9.4.3 — The capture must record, with the image: the timestamp from the server, the device identifier, the geo-coordinates where available, and the identity of the logged-in worker performing the application. The image is stored against that specific application, and its hash is recorded.
RULE 9.4.4 — The capture must be checked before it is accepted: a face must actually be present, the image must not be blank or blurred beyond use, and a liveness check must be applied so that a photograph of a photograph is rejected. If the check fails, the application cannot proceed, and the failure is explained to the worker.
RULE 9.4.5 — The system must detect attempted reuse: if the captured face matches a face already captured for a different customer, the application is flagged, the capture is recorded, and the responsible worker's manager is notified. This is the mechanism that makes "applying on behalf of someone who is not there" difficult and visible.
RULE 9.4.6 — The same law applies to the guarantor, with the same rules: live capture, no uploads, liveness check, metadata recorded, and a guarantor who is not present cannot be added.
RULE 9.4.7 — Face images are never editable, never replaceable by an upload, and are retained with the application permanently for audit. A re-application means a new capture.
RULE 9.4.8 — The customer's registration photograph follows the same live-capture rule, so the customer's record and the application face are both real captures of a person who was physically present.
9.5 The virtual account — when it exists
RULE 9.5.1 — The virtual account (VA) is generated at loan disbursement — not at customer registration, not at loan application, not at loan approval.
The exact current flow is:
C.O. registers the customer
  -> customer profile created (9.2)
  -> customer proceeds through the normal lending lifecycle
  -> loan application (live face capture, 9.4)
  -> approval chain
  -> loan disbursement
       -> the branch's provider creates the Virtual Account for that customer
       -> the VA is saved against that customer's record
       -> the VA is shown in the C.O.'s record for that customer and to every
          authorised user who can see the customer
       -> Customer Portal access is generated for the customer
       -> the customer receives the portal address and initial credentials
  -> the customer logs in and sees their actual records
RULE 9.5.2 — There is no rule that every registered customer receives a permanent virtual account at onboarding, and there is no requirement that a registered customer must have a virtual account before receiving a loan. The another version of that rule is withdrawn, must not be implemented, and must not be preserved alongside this one (Part 19 lists the removal).
RULE 9.5.3 — A customer cannot be disbursed without a working virtual account: if the provider call fails at disbursement, the disbursement is held in a clear, visible "virtual account pending" state, the branch and Finance are notified, and the system retries. The customer record is never rolled back or duplicated because of a provider outage.
RULE 9.5.4 — One customer, one current virtual account. If the provider must replace it, the old account's status becomes Replaced, it is never deleted, and the customer's current payment destination remains unambiguous.
RULE 9.5.5 — The virtual account record stores: company, branch, customer, provider, bank name, account name, account number, provider reference, creation timestamp, status (pending, active, replaced, closed), and the disbursement that created it.
9.6 Customer Portal access
RULE 9.6.1 — Customer Portal access is generated in the same step as the virtual account, at disbursement completion — not at registration, and not before the customer has a real loan and a real account to pay into.
RULE 9.6.2 — Generating access produces:
1.  The customer's username: the customer's full name (Part 5.1).
2.  The customer's initial password: at-sign plus first name (Part 5.2).
3.  The portal address the customer should use, plus the company's identifier so the customer's login resolves the right company.
4.  The one-time credential panel, shown to the worker who completed the disbursement, with a copy action.
RULE 9.6.3 — The customer logs in and completes the same credential ritual required of workers: change password, and set up and verify an authenticator where the company requires it for customers.
RULE 9.6.4 — Authenticator requirement for customers: on by default, because the customer's portal shows real money and real balances. The MD may switch it off for his company where his customer base genuinely cannot support an authenticator application, and the choice is recorded and audit-logged when changed. Changing the password is never optional for anybody, customer or worker.
RULE 9.6.5 — Customer access is never a "share the account with the officer" arrangement: the customer's credentials belong to the customer, and no worker ever logs in as a customer.
9.7 The Customer Portal
RULE 9.7.1 — The Customer Portal shows the customer's actual records, and nothing else. It contains, according to the customer's real data:
			Area
			Contents

			Profile
			The customer's photograph, personal details, contact and address

			Loan
			Loan product, approved amount, approved date, term and cycle

			Disbursement
			Amount disbursed, date, and the loan's status

			Balance
			Outstanding balance, total paid, total remaining

			Repayment schedule
			Every due date, the expected loan repayment for that date, and the actual posted payment allocation(s) applied against it, including any Savings Achieved resulting from those allocations

			Payments
			Every payment received, with date, amount and reference

			Receipts
			A receipt for each payment, downloadable

			Digital ledger
			The customer's own collection ledger, showing scheduled versus received

			Virtual account
			The account number, bank and account name to pay into, with a copy action

			Savings
			Savings Achieved from actual verified payments allocated and posted through the loan's repayment flow

			Notifications
			Payment received, payment due, overdue warning, and messages the company sends

			Support
			How to reach the company: the branch phone number or a message to the Collection Officer

RULE 9.7.2 — The portal is read-only except for the payments the customer makes to the provided account and any messages or profile updates the company allows. The customer never edits a payment, a balance or a schedule.
9.8 The no-fake-data law
RULE 9.8.1 — There are no fake records, no fake balances, no fake payment history, no sample customers, no demonstration amounts and no placeholder tiles in any live company or customer view. A screen with nothing to show says so honestly.
RULE 9.8.2 — A customer's portal is not the place for marketing figures. It shows the customer's own numbers, or it shows an empty state.
9.9 Customer notifications and the escalation rule
RULE 9.9.1 — The system must raise notifications for: payment received (to the customer and to the responsible worker), payment due soon, payment missed, overdue thresholds crossed, money received but awaiting allocation, and a customer's group falling silent.
RULE 9.9.2 — Where a customer's payment was received but not allocated in time, or a customer has not paid for a full window, the notification goes to both the responsible worker (usually the Collection Officer) and HR, because HR judges performance (Part 6.4.2).

PART 10 — THE LOAN LIFECYCLE
10.1 The lifecycle, stated once
Loan product defined (terms, cycle length, expected repayment and savings split,
                     approval chain, target profile)
   -> Customer registered (9.2)
   -> Loan application: live face capture of the applicant; documents; guarantor
        with live face capture; amount and product chosen
   -> Document collection stage
   -> Review stage
   -> Credit assessment stage (approve, reject, or request more information)
   -> Approval chain (configurable per product: e.g. C.O. submits, Branch Manager
        reviews, Credit Officer assesses, Credit Manager approves, GM or MD
        approves at final stage)
   -> Disbursement
        -> Virtual account generated and saved against the customer
        -> Customer Portal access generated
        -> Repayment schedule generated in full, one row per due date
   -> Active loan: payments arrive by transfer to the virtual account only
   -> Payment pipeline: verified webhook, identification, allocation, ledger,
        performance, accounting, notification
   -> Overdue and default handling per the company's rules
   -> Completed (or restructured, or written off through the configured process)
10.2 The loan application
RULE 10.2.1 — The application captures: the loan product, the requested amount, the requested cycle, the purpose, the applicant's live-captured face, the guarantor's live-captured face, the guarantor's details, and any documents the company's product configuration requires.
RULE 10.2.2 — There is no passport-number field and no passport photograph upload in the application. The face capture is the identification.
RULE 10.2.3 — The application records which worker submitted it, from which device, at which location, and at what time, so that a fraudulent application can always be traced to the person who submitted it.
RULE 10.2.4 — An application cannot proceed to review without a successful face capture and a successful guarantor capture.
10.3 The approval chain
RULE 10.3.1 — The company defines one or more approval chain templates, each an ordered list of roles (not people) that an application must pass through. A loan product is attached to a chain, so different products can use different routes.
RULE 10.3.2 — At each stage, only a holder of that stage's role, at the correct scope, can move the application forward. A Branch Manager of another branch cannot act on it.
RULE 10.3.3 — Every stage transition is audit-logged: actor, role assignment used, previous status, new status, timestamp, and the reason where required. Rejection always requires a reason.
RULE 10.3.4 — A rejection returns the application to a defined previous status (back to the applicant, or back one stage) as configured, never into a dead end.
RULE 10.3.5 — The MD holds the final say where the chain says so, and the default chain for a company that changes nothing ends at the MD.
10.4 Disbursement
RULE 10.4.1 — Disbursement happens only after the full chain approves, and only when: the customer's profile is complete, the face captures exist, the virtual account is created successfully, and the repayment schedule has been generated.
RULE 10.4.2 — Disbursement generates, atomically: the virtual account record, the customer portal access, the complete repayment schedule, the ledger, and the audit entries. If any part fails, the disbursement is held in a visible pending state with a clear explanation, and is never half-completed.
RULE 10.4.3 — Disbursement authority is the end of the configured approval chain, so a company may allow branch-level disbursement or keep it at Head Office. What may never change is the no-cash rule and the no-manual-entry rule.
10.5 Payments, allocation and the ledger
RULE 10.5.1 — Every payment arrives by bank transfer to the customer's virtual account, reaches the system as a verified provider webhook, is identified by the virtual account, is allocated by the C.O. Payment Allocation workflow, is written to the digital collection ledger, updates performance, updates accounting, and notifies the customer and the responsible worker. Verification and recording are automatic; allocation is not automatic and must be completed manually by the C.O.
RULE 10.5.2 — The repayment schedule is generated once, in full, at disbursement: one row per due date, each carrying the expected repayment and the configured savings component/split for that schedule date, per the product's split.
RULE 10.5.3 — The C.O. Payment Allocation workflow applies the permitted allocation rules. The C.O. chooses the allocation between loan repayment and savings, and Nexora validates that the total allocation equals the exact Verified Payment amount. Under-allocation, over-allocation, residuals, silent rounding, roll-forward and automatic correction are rejected.
RULE 10.5.3A — The C.O. dashboard contains a real Payment Allocation queue with a live pending count, for example “Payment Allocation — 3 Pending”. The queue columns are Customer, Group, Payment Ref, Received Amount, Loan Repayment, Savings and Status. A newly eligible item has status Awaiting Allocation.
RULE 10.5.3B — Opening a pending payment shows: Customer, Group, Payment Reference, received date/time, Verified Amount (locked), Loan Repayment input, Savings input, Total Allocation, Verified Payment and Remaining to Allocate. The financial context also shows the customer's outstanding loan balance, current/next expected repayment, current savings balance and the new verified payment.
RULE 10.5.3C — The allocation calculation is exact: Verified Payment − Total Allocation = Remaining to Allocate. Posting is enabled only when Loan Repayment + Savings = Verified Payment and Remaining to Allocate = 0. No hidden residual, silent rounding, automatic correction or unexplained adjustment is allowed.
RULE 10.5.3D — Example only: a verified payment of ₦7,000 may be allocated as ₦6,500 loan repayment + ₦500 savings = ₦7,000, leaving ₦0. An attempted ₦40,000 + ₦9,000 against ₦50,000 or ₦50,000 + ₦5,000 against ₦50,000 is rejected and cannot post.
RULE 10.5.3E — After successful posting, the payment moves through Awaiting Allocation → Allocated → Posted; it disappears from the pending queue but remains permanently available in customer, payment, loan, savings, ledger, accounting, report and audit history.
RULE 10.5.4 — The ledger's actual amounts are written only by the pipeline. No human writes a ledger figure, ever.
RULE 10.5.5 — A provider transaction that has been received and verified at the provider/integration level but cannot yet be matched or made eligible for C.O. allocation — for example, an unmatchable reference, a customer with no active loan, a duplicate, or a partial amount the company's rules do not resolve — is placed in the appropriate awaiting-allocation or reconciliation-exception state, with a visible reason and an owner. It is not treated as an eligible C.O. allocation item until internal eligibility succeeds. It is never silently dropped, never guessed at, and never force-fitted to a random loan.
RULE 10.5.5A — Payment exceptions must explicitly support at least: duplicate webhook, invalid webhook/signature, delayed webhook, provider downtime/retry, unmatched payment, unallocated payment, incomplete processing, reversed transaction, refund, chargeback/dispute and reconciliation mismatch. Each exception has a reason, owner, status, timestamps and audit trail. Invalid, refunded, reversed or chargebacked transactions cannot enter the normal C.O. queue.
RULE 10.5.6 — Money held while a portfolio is on hold (Part 5.8.2) sits in the same awaiting-allocation state until the portfolio is taken over, and remains visible to Head Office throughout.
10.6 Overdue, default and recovery
RULE 10.6.1 — Overdue begins when a scheduled amount passes its due date and the company's grace rule. Default begins when the company's default rule is crossed. Both are computed by the performance engine, never by a human.
RULE 10.6.2 — Overdue cases escalate: the responsible worker, then the Branch Manager, then recovery, then credit, then the MD, according to thresholds and the company's configuration.
RULE 10.6.3 — Restructuring and write-off are configured processes with their own approval chain; they create new records and never edit the original loan history.
10.7 Savings, as an outcome
RULE 10.7.1 — Savings appears in this product in exactly these ways, and no others:
1.  As a savings component/split in the repayment schedule, where the loan product defines it.
2.  As realised savings when payments arrive and the C.O. allocates the savings portion after payment verification.
3.  As savings achieved, shown in the customer's record, the Collection Officer's view of that customer, the branch's view, the company's view and the customer's portal.
4.  As a reportable figure in branch and company performance.
RULE 10.7.2 — There is no savings account product, no savings-only customer, no deposit-taking flow independent of a loan, and no savings-related role. If a company wants to record a saving, it is recorded as part of a customer's normal loan-and-payment lifecycle.
10.8 Loan-related prohibitions
RULE 10.8.1 — The following must not exist: a manual repayment or collection entry; a cash collection record; a "record what the customer paid me" screen; an offline payment entry that is later typed in; a loan application without a live face capture; a loan approval by a person outside the configured chain; a disbursement without a virtual account; a second customer record created for a loan; and any flow that advances a loan using a figure a human typed.
10.9 The traceability chain
RULE 10.9.1 — Every link in this chain is a real relationship in the data, not a report-time guess:
Payment -> Webhook -> Transaction -> Customer -> Virtual account -> Loan
        -> Allocation -> Ledger -> Accounting -> Branch -> Collection Officer
        -> Audit trail
From any node, an authorised user can click to the adjacent node, and the Auditor can walk the entire chain in either direction.

PART 11 — MONEY TRUTH, PERFORMANCE AND VISIBILITY
11.1 The binding vocabulary
RULE 11.1.1 — Every dashboard, report and API response in Nexora must use these exact definitions. No page, role or module may define its own version of a calculation.
			Term
			Definition

			Expected
			The amount scheduled as due for the selected date or period from the customer's loan repayment schedule. A configured savings component may exist in loan terms, but it is not Savings Achieved until an actual payment is allocated and posted.

			Actual / Realised
			The verified amount actually received and allocated for that date or period, sourced exclusively from the payment pipeline; never a typed figure

			Outstanding
			Expected minus Actual, for the portion still unpaid as of now, inside the current not-yet-overdue period

			Overdue / Default
			The amount still unpaid after the due date and the company's grace and overdue rules have passed

			Collection Rate
			Actual divided by Expected, times one hundred, with the company's configured handling of a period where nothing was due, so a branch with no collections due does not display a misleading zero

			Savings Achieved / Actual Posted Savings
			The actual savings amount resulting only from verified payments that have been allocated and posted to savings. It is never a scheduled, expected, forecast, target or projected savings amount.

			Actual Savings Variance
			If an actual savings variance is reported, it must be derived only from documented posted savings transactions against the applicable configured loan/product terms or another explicitly documented actual baseline. It must never be presented as a forecast, target or projected savings metric.

			Customers Expected
			How many customers had a scheduled loan repayment due for the period. Savings Achieved is measured only from actual posted payment allocations.

			Customers Paid
			How many of those customers paid, fully or partially, according to the company's "counts as paid" rule

			Customers Missed
			Customers Expected minus Customers Paid

RULE 11.1.2 — These words mean the same thing whether the figure is for one Collection Officer, one group, one branch or the whole company. The words never change meaning depending on which screen is showing them.
11.2 The performance engine and its hierarchy
RULE 11.2.1 — There is exactly one performance engine in Nexora. Every screen that shows expected, realised, outstanding, overdue, collection rate, savings figures or customer counts reads from it. No screen computes its own.
Customer -> Group -> Collection Officer -> Branch -> Area (where used) -> Company
RULE 11.2.2 — At every level the same figures are calculated with the same definitions, and the totals must reconcile downwards:
•  Branch total equals the sum of its underlying customer and loan records.
•  Company total equals the sum of all its branches.
•  A worker's total equals the sum of his assigned customers and groups.
•  An area total equals the sum of its branches.
RULE 11.2.3 — Figures update the moment a verified payment clears the pipeline. A payment received at 2:14pm appears in the responsible worker's figures, his branch's figures and the company's figures immediately, not the next day.
RULE 11.2.4 — Because everything derives from the pipeline and the schedules, and because no manual collection entry exists anywhere, no figure in the product can be distorted by a typed number.

11.4 No manual money, no editing, reversal only
RULE 11.4.1 — There is no manual payment-creation entry in Nexora. A C.O. may enter allocation values only for an already-verified payment whose received amount is locked by the system.
RULE 11.4.2 — There is no edit control over money anywhere in the product: not on a payment, an allocation, a balance, a ledger line, an accounting entry or a virtual account.
RULE 11.4.3 — Financial corrections use a controlled reversal/correction workflow that creates new linked audited records and leaves the original record permanently intact. The only roles that may approve a financial reversal/correction are MD, GM and authorised Auditor, subject to the company's configured control policy. Finance may investigate and prepare the request but cannot approve or post it. Once all required approvals exist, the system—not a person editing the original—creates the new reversal/correction record.
11.5 Reconciliation and the exception queue
RULE 11.5.1 — Reconciliation is a standing module, not a report. It holds a live, resolvable list of: unallocated payments, unmatched payments, payments found on the provider side but missing internally, duplicate webhooks, reversed transactions, failed webhook processing and incomplete disbursements.
RULE 11.5.2 — Each exception has an owner, an age, a reason, a status and a resolution note. Nothing is closed without a note.
RULE 11.5.3 — The exception queue is worked by authorised Finance users for reconciliation and resolution preparation, watched by Auditors and visible to MD/GM. Finance cannot edit financial truth or approve/post a reversal unless the person is separately an MD, GM or authorised Auditor under the control model.
11.6 Accounting and reports
RULE 11.6.1 — Accounting statements are generated automatically from the verified pipeline and the ledger: general ledger, cash book, cash flow, income and financial position. The terms "cash book" and "cash flow" are standard accounting terms only; Nexora has no physical cash handling anywhere.
RULE 11.6.2 — The reporting system produces exportable reports, filterable by period, branch, role and worker, drawing exclusively on the same verified data, with branch performance, worker performance and collection officer performance as defined report types.
RULE 11.6.3 — No report may contain a figure that cannot be traced back to a payment, a schedule or an audit entry.

PART 13 — THE COLOUR, LOGO AND VISUAL LAW
No design has been produced yet (see the note at the top of this document). This part is not a design; it is the law the design must obey, and the exact list of what changes when a company chooses its colour.
13.1 The philosophy
RULE 13.1.1 — The company's colour is a highlighter, not a background wash. It is applied to a small, defined set of elements — the things a user must look at to act, or to know where they are — and it is deliberately absent from everything else.
WHY — if the interface is drenched in the company colour, the colour stops meaning anything, text becomes hard to read, and every screen looks like a warning. The colour must mean "this is the action" or "this is where you are".
RULE 13.1.2 — Backgrounds, surfaces, cards, tables and paragraphs are neutral and layered, and they never change when a company colour is chosen. Only the defined accent tokens change.

PART 14 — SECURITY AND ACCOUNT-STATE LAW
14.1 The authentication model
RULE 14.1.1 — Every account authenticates with a username and password, and every account must additionally hold a verified authenticator where the role or policy requires it. Authenticator verification is mandatory for every worker account (all roles, all companies), the MD, the Platform Owner, and any account performing a sensitive action: password change, authenticator change, credential re-issue, portfolio hold, portfolio transfer, provider change, or a high-value reversal.
14.2 The authenticator
RULE 14.2.1 — An authenticator is bound to an account only after a successful live verification. Setup alone never counts.
RULE 14.2.2 — HR (or the MD) can freeze an account's authenticator. The moment it is frozen it stops working immediately, along with the password, and the account cannot log in from anywhere outside the company (Part 5.8).
RULE 14.2.3 — HR (or the MD) can remove an authenticator as part of a hold or a transfer. Removal is audit-logged, and the account cannot become usable again without a fresh, documented credential issue and a fresh ritual.
RULE 14.2.4 — No user can remove or re-set another person's authenticator, and no user can remove their own authenticator without going through the password-plus-authenticator verification path.
14.3 The audit field set
RULE 14.3.1 — Every audit entry carries: the user, the role assignment used, the company, the branch where applicable, the server timestamp, the action, the previous value, the new value, the reason where the action requires one, the transaction reference where the action concerns money, and the device and IP metadata where available.
RULE 14.3.2 — Audit entries are append-only. There is no edit and no delete action anywhere, for anyone, including the Platform Owner.
RULE 14.3.3 — Security and account-state transitions are audited as a distinct, always-on class (Part 5.10): credential issue, consumption, expiry, password change, authenticator setup, verification, freeze, removal, profile completion, worker create and edit, passport replacement, role assignment changes, suspension, reactivation, termination, portfolio hold, hold release, portfolio transfer, login success, login failure and lockout.
14.4 Sessions, devices and lockout
RULE 14.4.1 — Sessions expire after the company's configured idle timeout, and sensitive actions require re-verification with the authenticator.
RULE 14.4.2 — Repeated failed logins lock the account for a cool-down period, and the lockout is audit-logged and notified to HR and the MD for worker accounts.
RULE 14.4.3 — Suspending a company, suspending a branch, freezing a worker or transferring a portfolio invalidates the affected sessions immediately.
14.5 Secret isolation
RULE 14.5.1 — Provider credentials, webhook secrets and authenticator seeds are encrypted at rest, are never logged, never exported, never displayed after saving except through an explicit authorised reveal, and never included in support data.
RULE 14.5.2 — A company's secrets are inaccessible to every other company, and company-side pages never expose platform secrets.

PART 16 — THE BUILD ORDER (BACKEND FIRST, THEN DESIGN INSTRUCTIONS, THEN FRONT END)
16.0 The order of work, and why
RULE 16.0.1 — The build proceeds in the order below, and the order is not negotiable:
1.  Backend first, complete. Databases, services, rules, credentials, providers, webhooks, permissions, audit, isolation, reports data — all of it finished and working, verified by automated tests and by hand.
2.  Then the Vision Audit. A dedicated stage in which the builder audits his own work, line by line, against this document, finds every deviation, and fixes it. Nothing is carried into design with a known deviation.
3.  Then the design instructions document. Only after the backend is complete and audited does the builder write Cornelius Nexora Finance design instructions for a separate chat to design the front end from (Part 17 specifies that document).
4.  Then the front end is built, from those instructions, and the builder implements it against the backend and corrects whatever the design exposes.
WHY this order — designing before the backend is finished produces screens for rules that do not exist yet, which is how products end up with beautiful pages that do not work. The backend is the truth; the design is how the truth is presented.
RULE 16.0.2 — No UI design deliverable, mock-up or style guide may be produced before Stage 13 (the Vision Audit) has passed.

PART 19 — GROUP LENDING AND COMPLETE LOAN APPLICATION LAW
19.1 Group creation and membership
RULE 19.1.1 — Collection Officers create and manage groups within their authorised branch scope. A group may contain two, three, or any larger number of members; there is no fixed member limit.
RULE 19.1.2 — Group creation requires Group Name, Group Number, Group Address and Date Created. The group name is chosen by the C.O. and is not limited to a predefined naming list.
RULE 19.1.3 — After creation, the group exposes Add Member. Each member is saved individually before the C.O. adds the next member.
RULE 19.1.4 — Member registration requires Full Name, Father/Husband, Marital Status selected from configured options, Phone Number and Group Role selected from Leader, Secretary, Treasurer, Chief Whip or Member. Role is selected, not free-typed.
RULE 19.1.5 — Group roles are organisational roles. A group does not own money, a loan, a savings balance or a ledger; all financial truth remains attached to the individual customer and loan records.
19.2 Active-loan gate and loan-cycle visibility
RULE 19.2.1 — On the customer page, Apply for Loan is available only when the customer has no active loan. A customer with an active loan must not be offered a new normal loan-application action through this flow.
RULE 19.2.2 — The customer page always shows the customer's current loan-cycle state where applicable. A returning customer is identified from actual prior loan records, and previous loan history remains available to authorised reviewers.
RULE 19.2.3 — A new application for a customer with completed prior loans creates a new loan record and loan cycle; it never creates a duplicate customer.
19.3 Daily versus weekly loan mode
RULE 19.3.1 — The first loan-term choice is repayment mode: Weekly or Daily. This selection is a functional mode, not a display label. It controls schedule generation, due dates, expected repayment calculations, overdue logic, notifications, performance calculations and relevant reports.
RULE 19.3.2 — For Weekly mode, the C.O. selects the applicable repayment weekday and enters the loan amount, interest percentage, repayment amount and repayment period in weeks. The system generates the resulting schedule.
RULE 19.3.3 — For Daily mode, the C.O. enters the configured daily repayment terms. The system generates the daily schedule and applies the same mathematical integrity controls.
RULE 19.3.4 — The C.O. enters the interest percentage, not the calculated overall repayment amount. Nexora calculates interest and overall contractual repayment from the actual configured loan terms.
RULE 19.3.5 — If principal is ₦100,000 and interest is 20%, Nexora calculates ₦20,000 interest and ₦120,000 overall contractual repayment. The example is illustrative only and is never hard-coded.
RULE 19.3.6 — If weekly repayment is ₦5,000 for 24 weeks, Nexora calculates ₦120,000 and compares it with the calculated overall contractual repayment. If the totals match, the stage is valid. If they do not match, the application is flagged NOT TALLY and cannot proceed.
RULE 19.3.7 — The same exact-total rule applies to Daily loans: repayment amount multiplied by the configured number of repayment periods must reconcile exactly with the calculated contractual repayment, subject to explicitly documented product rules. No silent rounding, residual or correction is permitted.
RULE 19.3.8 — Where a monthly-equivalent duration is displayed, the software derives it from the configured repayment period; the C.O. does not manually calculate or type a misleading month duration.
19.4 Customer and guarantor information before evidence capture
RULE 19.4.1 — The application captures the required customer information, including Next of Kin, Occupation, House Address, Street, Direction to Locate the House, Child Name where applicable, Local/Area Known-As Name, Shop Address, Average Daily Income and Average Monthly Income.
RULE 19.4.2 — Customer information must be saved before the application proceeds to the next stage.
RULE 19.4.3 — The guarantor is a first-class application party with its own required information and evidence. The system must not treat the guarantor as a single text field or a decorative attachment.
19.5 Live face capture — customer and guarantor
RULE 19.5.1 — Customer and guarantor face captures are live camera captures. Gallery uploads, arbitrary image uploads, skipped captures and reuse of an unrelated prior capture are prohibited for the live-face requirement.
RULE 19.5.2 — The face capture must pass configured quality controls including sufficient brightness, clarity, face presence and liveness. If the image is too dark, unclear, unusable or fails the configured liveness/quality checks, Nexora rejects it and requires another capture.
RULE 19.5.3 — Live capture is not limited to one image. The workflow must support Add Capture wherever additional evidence is useful or required. Every accepted capture is an independent immutable evidence record.
RULE 19.5.4 — Each capture records the application, customer/guarantor identity, capture type, timestamp, capturing worker, device information where available, location where available, file metadata and integrity hash.
19.6 Resumable application and stage checkpoints
RULE 19.6.1 — A loan application is a persistent state machine. Leaving the application never deletes the application or silently resets its stage.
RULE 19.6.2 — Before Next is enabled for a stage, that stage must be successfully saved to the server. The application records the last successfully saved stage and its state.
RULE 19.6.3 — Draft data should be auto-saved where technically appropriate, but the authoritative stage checkpoint is the successful server-side save. If the network fails before server confirmation, the UI must not claim that the stage is saved.
RULE 19.6.4 — When the C.O. returns later, the application opens at the last saved/resumable stage. Customer and guarantor stages may be completed at different times and locations.
RULE 19.6.5 — A failed or interrupted upload must be represented as Pending/Failed rather than falsely marked Saved. A protected local retry mechanism may retain an encrypted pending capture until successful server upload, subject to device security and retention policy.
19.7 Government ID, name matching and house evidence
RULE 19.7.1 — After face stages, the workflow captures the customer government ID live and saves it, then captures the guarantor government ID live and saves it.
RULE 19.7.2 — The customer application name and government-ID name must pass the configured identity-matching rule. A mismatch is immediately flagged and cannot silently pass as a match. The same applies to the guarantor.
RULE 19.7.3 — House live capture is the front view of the house. Customer house capture comes first, followed by guarantor house capture. Multiple house captures are permitted for each party.
RULE 19.7.4 — Each house capture is saved as a separate evidence record and may include additional views required to establish the location or property context.
19.8 Business evidence
RULE 19.8.1 — Business live capture follows house capture: customer business first, guarantor business second. Multiple captures are permitted for each party.
RULE 19.8.2 — The system must not assume one business image is sufficient. Each additional accepted capture is separately stored and traceable.
19.9 Fees and bank details
RULE 19.9.1 — After the required live evidence stages, the application records the Overall Loan Fee Paid and any other configured loan-related fee fields. These fees are separate from principal and calculated interest.
RULE 19.9.1A — The application may capture the configured loan-fee obligation and its status for workflow purposes, but a C.O. cannot manually create a financial payment or use a typed fee value as proof that money was received. A fee becomes financial truth only when supported by the verified provider-payment pipeline and its resulting allocation/posting.
RULE 19.9.2 — Customer bank details required for disbursement include Bank Name, Bank Account Number and Name on Account.
RULE 19.9.3 — The bank account name must correspond with the customer application identity and government-ID identity according to the configured matching rule. A mismatch is immediately flagged and must not silently proceed.
19.10 Loan-form and default-form evidence
RULE 19.10.1 — Loan-form live capture supports multiple captures/pages. The C.O. can capture, save and Add Capture until all required form evidence is complete.
RULE 19.10.2 — Default-form live capture follows the same multi-capture, save-before-next principle.
19.11 Final C.O. preview and submission
RULE 19.11.1 — Before submission, Nexora presents a complete application preview containing the customer, guarantor, group, loan cycle, Daily/Weekly mode, loan terms, calculated repayment, schedule, fees, bank details, identity validation status and all captured evidence.
RULE 19.11.2 — The C.O. submits only after required stages and validations pass. Submission changes the application into the configured approval workflow; it does not delete the draft history.
19.12 Configurable approval workflow
RULE 19.12.1 — At company creation/configuration, the company selects its loan-disbursement approval chain and the roles that participate. The platform must support different valid chains without requiring a new frontend design for each role.
RULE 19.12.2 — The configured chain may include C.O., Branch Manager, Area Manager, Auditor roles, HR or other authorised roles, followed by the final disbursement authority, subject to the company configuration and role authority model. The platform must not hard-code one universal sequence.
RULE 19.12.3 — If an intermediate configured role is unavailable for the relevant branch/area, Nexora resolves the workflow to the next applicable configured stage. It must not stop a loan merely because a branch has no Branch Manager or the relevant state has no Area Manager. A stage explicitly marked Mandatory/Non-Skippable by the company's approval configuration cannot be bypassed merely because its assignee is unavailable. If an Auditor or other final pre-disbursement approval stage is configured as mandatory, that stage must be resolved by an authorised assignee or an explicit company-authorised reassignment path before disbursement can proceed.
RULE 19.12.4 — Each approval role receives a dedicated approval workspace automatically when that role is selected in the company workflow. The existing role dashboard does not need to be redesigned manually for every possible workflow combination.
RULE 19.12.5 — The approval workspace shows action-required counts. One waiting approval produces 1 Action Required; two produce 2 Actions Required; and so on. Notifications appear as pop-ups and in the in-app notification centre.
RULE 19.12.6 — Opening an approval item must open a persistent review workspace, not a destructive temporary popup. Closing/back navigation never deletes the underlying application. The item remains pending until a real workflow action changes its state.
RULE 19.12.7 — Every approver can preview the full authorised application package, including customer/guarantor details, group, loan cycle, previous loan history, repayment history, savings balance where an actual balance exists, evidence captures, bank/identity checks, fees, schedule and all other relevant submitted records.
RULE 19.12.8 — Approval can be Approve or Decline, with Decline requiring a reason. If the configured workflow supports Return for Information, that is a separate auditable state and is not disguised as rejection.
RULE 19.12.9 — Chief Auditor and other configured auditors may receive an approval stage concurrently where the company workflow defines an auditor approval group. Their actions remain individually attributable and auditable.
19.13 Final disbursement
RULE 19.13.1 — The final disbursement authority receives the same complete review context before the Disburse action becomes available.
RULE 19.13.2 — Disbursement is available only after all required approval stages have completed and all technical prerequisites have succeeded.
RULE 19.13.3 — Clicking Disburse triggers the real backend disbursement workflow, including applicable Virtual Account creation, repayment schedule creation, customer portal/access creation, loan activation, financial/accounting records and audit records. The UI must never simulate successful disbursement.
RULE 19.13.4 — The responsible C.O. is mandatorily notified when the loan is approved, declined, returned for information or disbursed, according to the actual state transition.
19.14 Customer loan history and post-disbursement operation
RULE 19.14.1 — Once disbursed, the customer page shows the active loan, loan cycle, repayment mode, schedule, actual payments, outstanding balance, overdue state and actual posted savings where applicable.
RULE 19.14.2 — A completed loan remains permanently in the customer history. If the customer later has no active loan, Apply for Loan becomes available and a new loan cycle is created without creating a new customer record.
RULE 19.14.3 — Existing savings is calculated from actual posted savings records and is never typed by the C.O. If the customer has an actual savings balance, authorised approval users may see it in the new application. If no savings balance exists, the application does not fabricate one.
RULE 19.14.4 — Where the loan terms include a configured savings component, actual savings increases only when a verified payment is allocated to savings and posted. A scheduled savings component is not Savings Achieved until an actual verified payment is allocated and posted.
19.15 Group collection and ledger presentation
RULE 19.15.1 — The C.O. dashboard must make the group an operational collection unit. Each group shows its members and each member's current loan, loan cycle, repayment schedule, expected repayment, actual posted repayment, remaining balance, savings achieved and overdue position where applicable.
RULE 19.15.2 — The system must update balances immediately after an approved payment allocation is posted. Loan balance decreases by the posted loan-repayment allocation; savings balance increases by the posted savings allocation.
RULE 19.15.3 — Group and customer views may resemble the operational usefulness of the provided weekly loan ledger, but every displayed number must come from real Nexora records and the single calculation engine. Spreadsheet formulas must not be copied as hidden manual logic.
PART 20 — DATA STORAGE, LIVE-CAPTURE INTEGRITY AND DISASTER RECOVERY LAW
20.1 Separation of financial data and evidence files
RULE 20.1.1 — PostgreSQL (or the selected authoritative relational database) stores the authoritative business, financial, workflow, identity metadata, audit and relationship records. Large images/documents are stored in encrypted object storage with immutable object identity and database metadata linking each object to its record.
RULE 20.1.2 — Each evidence object stores at minimum company_id, branch_id, customer_id, loan_application_id where applicable, party (customer/guarantor), capture type, capture sequence, storage object reference, file hash, file size, MIME type, captured_by, captured_at, device metadata where available, location where available and verification status.
RULE 20.1.3 — Evidence is never silently overwritten. A new capture creates a new immutable evidence record; the previous record remains available to authorised audit/review users.
20.2 Upload and save truth
RULE 20.2.1 — The client must never display Saved until the server has durably accepted the data/object and returned a successful authoritative save response.
RULE 20.2.2 — Failed, interrupted or pending uploads must have explicit states. The system must not convert a client-side capture into a financial or evidence record until the server confirms persistence.
RULE 20.2.3 — Where offline capture is supported, pending captures must be protected locally and retried safely; duplicate uploads are prevented by idempotency and content/object identifiers. A pending local item is never treated as a completed server record.
20.3 Financial immutability
RULE 20.3.1 — Payments, allocations, disbursements, loan balances, savings postings, accounting entries, approval decisions, provider transactions and audit events are not destructively deleted or rewritten through ordinary application operations.
RULE 20.3.2 — Corrections use reversal/authorized-correction records that reference the original record. The original historical record remains intact.
RULE 20.3.3 — No administrator, MD, Finance user, Accountant, IT user or developer-facing maintenance path may be used to silently rewrite financial history.
20.4 Backup architecture
RULE 20.4.1 — Production PostgreSQL must have encrypted continuous transaction/WAL backup capability and point-in-time recovery appropriate to the agreed production RPO.
RULE 20.4.2 — Scheduled full database backups must be encrypted and stored separately from the primary database infrastructure.
RULE 20.4.3 — Evidence/object storage must have its own encrypted backup/replication strategy. A database-only backup is not considered a complete Nexora backup.
RULE 20.4.4 — At least one protected backup copy must be isolated from ordinary application credentials and protected with appropriate retention/immutability controls against accidental deletion and destructive incidents.
RULE 20.4.5 — Backup retention, geographic placement and data residency must be configurable according to the production infrastructure and applicable legal/company requirements.
20.5 Recovery and restore testing
RULE 20.5.1 — Nexora must define an RPO and RTO before production launch. These are engineering acceptance criteria, not informal aspirations.
RULE 20.5.2 — Backups must be periodically restored into a controlled recovery environment. A backup that has never been successfully restored is not treated as proven disaster recovery.
RULE 20.5.3 — Recovery testing must verify companies, branches, customers, groups, loans, loan cycles, schedules, payments, allocations, savings, accounting, approvals, audit records, provider transactions and evidence-object integrity.
RULE 20.5.4 — Evidence hashes must be checked during recovery so corruption or mismatch is detected rather than silently accepted.
20.6 Reconciliation after recovery or incident
RULE 20.6.1 — After a restore or major incident, Nexora runs consistency checks across the authoritative database and evidence storage before declaring the environment recovered.
RULE 20.6.2 — Payment allocation must reconcile exactly: verified payment = loan allocation + savings allocation. Loan and savings balances must reconcile from actual posted records. Provider transactions must reconcile to Nexora payment records.
RULE 20.6.3 — Any unreconciled item becomes an explicit exception requiring investigation. Nexora must never invent a balance or silently adjust money to make totals appear correct.
PART 21 — READ-ONLY COMPANY AI LAW AND ROLE-INTELLIGENCE REQUIREMENTS
21.1 Tenant-isolated AI
RULE 21.1.1 — Each company may have an AI assistant inside its Nexora environment. The AI operates under the authenticated user identity, company tenant, branch scope, role and permissions of that user.
RULE 21.1.2 — Company A AI cannot retrieve, infer from or expose Company B data. Tenant isolation is enforced by the data-access layer before information is supplied to the AI context.
RULE 21.1.3 — The AI is read-only. It cannot edit customer records, loan terms, payments, allocations, balances, accounting, approvals, salaries, provider configuration or any other authoritative record.
RULE 21.1.4 — AI answers about company data must be grounded in actual authorised Nexora records. If the required data is unavailable, ambiguous or outside the user's permission, the AI must say so rather than invent an answer.
RULE 21.1.5 — AI-generated letters and notifications must be based on actual performance/financial records and documented company rules. The AI may draft communication, but the underlying financial system remains authoritative.
21.1.6 OpenCode is the required AI provider/model integration and execution layer
RULE 21.1.6 — Nexora's company AI capability must use OpenCode as the real provider/model integration and execution layer. This is a real backend integration, not a mock, UI-only selector, simulated response system or hard-coded provider catalogue.
RULE 21.1.7 — Nexora must not hard-code a fixed list of AI providers or provider models. OpenCode is the source for the dynamic provider/model catalogue used by Nexora. The application must obtain the currently available provider catalogue through the real OpenCode integration and must obtain the latest available models from the selected provider through that real integration.
RULE 21.1.8 — OpenCode must be properly installed and available to the platform's execution architecture. The integration must use real OpenCode capability and real provider/model execution. Fake OpenCode responses, fake provider availability, fake model availability and placeholder provider implementations are prohibited.
RULE 21.1.9 — When the Platform Owner selects an AI provider and model for a company, that exact provider/model becomes the selected AI configuration for that company. The configuration must be persisted and must flow into the real backend AI execution path. The company AI must not silently use a different provider or model.
RULE 21.1.10 — Provider/model configuration is company-scoped. Company A may use one provider/model while Company B uses another. A provider/model selection, API key, verification state, execution request, response or error for one company must never be used for or exposed to another company.
RULE 21.1.11 — Provider/API credentials used for company AI are handled securely. Secrets must be encrypted at rest where persisted, masked in the interface, excluded from logs, excluded from ordinary API responses and excluded from error messages. The exact storage/handling mechanism must preserve the existing security and secret-isolation laws of this Vision.
RULE 21.1.12 — Verify is a real verification. It must verify the entered provider, API key and selected model through the real OpenCode/provider integration rather than merely checking that fields are populated. A successful verification may only be shown after a real successful verification. A failed verification must report the real failure and must not mark the configuration as working.
RULE 21.1.13 — A company's currently active AI configuration must not be replaced by an unverified provider/model configuration. A new provider/model becomes active only after successful real verification and explicit completion of the Done/save action. Until then, the previously active verified configuration remains the active configuration, unless the user explicitly removes/disables it through an authorized flow.
RULE 21.1.14 — If a selected provider/model becomes unavailable, unsupported or fails during execution, Nexora must surface the actual failure and use the appropriate failure/recovery mechanism. It must never substitute a fake response or silently switch to an unrelated provider/model.
RULE 21.1.15 — The rest of Nexora must communicate with the AI capability through a clean OpenCode integration boundary. OpenCode is the actual provider/model integration and execution layer used now. Nexora must not create separate provider-specific AI integrations that bypass OpenCode where OpenCode provides the required provider/model capability. Provider-specific assumptions must not be scattered through the application; provider/model discovery, connection handling and execution must remain behind the OpenCode integration boundary so the implementation can be replaced later without rewriting the product.

The model names shown in this Vision (including Space Bunny Free, MiMo-V2.6-Flash Free, Muse Spark 1.3 Free, Ling 3.0 Flash Fin Free, Nemotron 3.5 Lightning Free, Muse Spark 1.2 Free, Nemotron 3 Ultra Free and Big Pickle) are examples of the intended presentation only; they are not a fixed catalogue and must never be treated as permanently required models. If availability changes, the interface must reflect the current real OpenCode availability. A model must never be shown as available merely because its example name appears in this Vision.
RULE 21.2.3 — The Provider dropdown dynamically uses the real provider catalogue supplied through OpenCode. Providers must not be hard-coded into the frontend.
RULE 21.2.4 — After a provider is selected, the Model dropdown dynamically obtains the latest models available from that provider through the real integration. The model dropdown must not contain a permanently hard-coded provider model list. If a previously selected model is no longer returned as available, the application must show the real unavailable state and must not silently substitute another model.
RULE 21.2.5 — The API Key field is masked by default. The exact explanatory text must remain: "This key is stored locally and only used to make API requests from this application." In this Vision, "stored locally" means stored within Nexora's application-controlled secure secret storage for the configured company and used only for that company's AI requests; it does not mean browser localStorage, plaintext client storage, a shared/global credential store, or storage accessible to another company.
RULE 21.2.6 — The exact model explanatory text must remain: "The application automatically fetches the latest available models from the selected provider."
RULE 21.2.7 — Verify and Done remain on the same row, with Verify on the left and Done on the right. Verify performs the real verification defined by Rule 21.1.12. Done saves/accepts the verified configuration and closes the panel.
RULE 21.2.8 — The selected provider and model from this exact interface must flow into the actual company backend AI execution system. The interface must never be disconnected from execution.
RULE 21.2.9 — The company's selected provider, selected model, verification state and secure credential reference must be persisted against that exact company and must survive normal page reloads and application/server restarts. They must not exist only in browser/session state.
RULE 21.2.10 — If the interface reports a provider/model as connected and selected, the backend must actually be capable of using that provider/model. If verification succeeds, a real verification must have occurred. If a model is displayed as available, it must originate from the actual provider/model discovery system.
21.3 AI execution, search correctness and company concurrency
RULE 21.3.1 — Company AI requests must resolve the company from the authenticated request and use that company's persisted AI configuration. A request made inside Company A must never resolve Company B's provider/model configuration.
RULE 21.3.2 — Company search through AI must be grounded in the exact authorised company data available to that user. "Search" does not permit cross-company discovery. If a company user asks about customers, loans, payments, reports, workers, branches or other company information, the AI context must be constructed only from records the authenticated user is authorised to access.
RULE 21.3.3 — AI execution must use the same multi-company concurrency and isolation principles in Part 3.8. Simultaneous AI requests from many companies must be independently scoped and must not share mutable tenant context, secrets, prompts containing another company's data or provider/model selection.
RULE 21.3.4 — AI execution must not become a single global bottleneck that unnecessarily serialises unrelated company requests. The execution architecture must support concurrent requests, bounded resource use, provider-specific rate limits, retries/backoff where appropriate, cancellation/timeouts and failure isolation while preserving tenant and role boundaries.
RULE 21.3.5 — AI does not replace Nexora's normal backend execution. Financial operations, provider API calls, virtual-account creation, payment verification, payment allocation, accounting and other authoritative business operations remain deterministic backend/provider-integration operations governed by their respective Parts. AI is not permitted to perform those authoritative writes.
WHY — OpenCode provides the dynamic provider/model capability without forcing Nexora to implement every AI provider individually, while Nexora remains responsible for company isolation, permissions, persistence and authoritative business rules.
PROOF — Configure different provider/models for multiple companies, verify each configuration, issue concurrent authorised AI requests from those companies, and prove each request uses only its own company's configuration and authorised data. Verify that the exact AI MODEL interface is connected to the same backend configuration and execution path.
21.4 Role-aware intelligence and dashboards
RULE 21.4.1 — Each role dashboard must be designed from the role's actual responsibility, authority, work objects, exceptions, approvals, reports and required decisions, not by copying another role's dashboard.
RULE 21.4.2 — Auditors require deep search, financial traceability, accounting/audit views and exception investigation tools within their permissions. They must be able to trace figures back to source records.
RULE 21.4.3 — Branch Workplace must expose the authorised branch's actual work: C.O.s, groups, collection performance, amount expected for a selected day, amount realised for completed days, amount realised yet for the current day, exceptions, trends and branch-level financial/operational status, with historical date search.
RULE 21.4.4 — Performance comparisons and any branch ranking displayed to authorised roles must be based on explicitly documented actual-data metrics and configured company rules. The platform must not fabricate rankings or use an unexplained composite score.
RULE 21.4.5 — Performance-versus-salary monitoring remains configurable. Where thresholds are configured, Nexora may generate explanatory notifications/letters to the C.O., Branch Manager, Area Manager and HR as applicable. Such alerts do not themselves change compensation or employment status.
PART 22 — FINAL END-TO-END ACCEPTANCE
The Vision Audit and end-to-end acceptance suite must additionally prove the following without mock data being mistaken for production truth:
• A C.O. can create a group with the required fields and add unlimited members one by one.
• Group member roles are selected from the configured group-role options and are saved individually.
• Apply for Loan is hidden for an active-loan customer and available for a customer with no active loan.
• A returning customer receives the correct next loan cycle and prior-loan history is visible to authorised reviewers.
• Weekly mode asks for a repayment weekday and validates repayment amount × weeks against calculated total repayment.
• Daily mode generates a daily schedule and performs the same exact-total integrity validation.
• Interest and overall repayment are calculated by Nexora rather than manually entered as a total by the C.O.
• Identity/name mismatches between application, government ID and bank account are flagged and cannot silently pass.
• Customer and guarantor face captures require acceptable brightness/clarity/liveness and support multiple captures.
• Customer and guarantor can be completed at different times and the application resumes at the last saved stage.
• House captures are front-view evidence and support multiple captures for customer and guarantor.
• Business, loan-form and default-form captures support multiple saved captures/pages.
• An application stage cannot advance until its required server-side save succeeds.
• Final preview contains the full application, evidence, loan cycle, previous loan history and actual savings where a balance exists.
• The company-configured approval chain executes correctly, skips unavailable intermediate roles where permitted, and exposes approval workspaces dynamically to selected roles.
• Every approval stage provides full authorised review context, action-required notification, approve/decline actions and required decline reasons.
• Closing an approval view never deletes the application or removes the pending action.
• The final disbursement authority sees the full application before Disburse, and Disburse triggers real backend state changes.
• The responsible C.O. receives the required notification for approval, decline, return-for-information and disbursement outcomes.
• Posted payments update loan balance and savings from actual allocations, and the group ledger reflects the updated balances.
• All evidence and financial records survive application restarts, server failures and tested disaster-recovery restoration.
• No financial record can be destructively deleted; corrections are traceable reversals/authorized corrections.
• Company AI can answer authorised read-only questions from that company's real data and cannot cross tenant boundaries or mutate records.

MD, GM and authorised Auditors are the only financial-correction approvers under the configured control model. The system creates any approved reversal/correction as a new linked record and preserves the original record and complete audit trail.
